## **Zero Trust Model** 🔒

**"Never trust, always verify."** The **Zero Trust Model** assumes that threats exist **inside and outside** the network. Every access request must be **verified, authorized, and continuously monitored**—regardless of location or device.

---

### **🔹 Core Principles**

✅ **Verify Every Access Request** – No implicit trust, even for internal users.  
✅ **Least Privilege Access** – Grant only the minimum required permissions.  
✅ **Micro-Segmentation** – Divide networks to prevent lateral movement.  
✅ **Continuous Monitoring** – Detect unusual behavior and revoke access if needed.  
✅ **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)** – Require multiple verification steps.

---

### **🔹 Real-World Implementations**

🔹 **MFA for All Users** – Require extra verification for logins.  
🔹 **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)** – Limit access based on job roles.  
🔹 **Device Trust Verification** – Allow only approved devices to connect.  
🔹 **Micro-Segmentation in Kubernetes** – Restrict pod-to-pod communication.  
🔹 **Cloud Security (AWS/Azure)** – Use Identity and Access Management (IAM) with **Zero Trust policies**.

---

### **🔹 Benefits**

✅ **Prevents Unauthorized Access** – Stops attackers from exploiting trust.  
✅ **Reduces Attack Surface** – Restricts access across the network.  
✅ **Mitigates Insider Threats** – No one gets access by default.  
✅ **Enhances Compliance** – Meets **GDPR, HIPAA, and ISO 27001** standards.

---

### **🔹 Implementation in Cloud & DevOps**

🔹 **Use IAM roles & RBAC** for granular access control.  
🔹 **Enable network policies in Kubernetes** for micro-segmentation.  
🔹 **Deploy Identity Providers (IdP)** like Okta, Azure AD, or AWS IAM.  
🔹 **Enforce endpoint security** before allowing access to cloud resources.
